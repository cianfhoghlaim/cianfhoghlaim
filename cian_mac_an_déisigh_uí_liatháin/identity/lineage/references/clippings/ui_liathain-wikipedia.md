# Uí Liatháin

The **Uí Liatháin** (Irish pronunciation:[iːˈlʲiəhaːnʲ]) were an early kingdom of [Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster) in southern Ireland. They belonged the same kindred as the **[Uí Fidgenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C3%AD_Fidgenti)**, and the two are considered together in the earliest sources, for example *[The Expulsion of the Déisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Expulsion_of_the_D%C3%A9isi)* (incidentally). The two have been given various origins among both the early or proto-[Eóganachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E%C3%B3ganachta) and among the [Érainn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89rainn) or [Dáirine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1irine) by different scholars working in a number of traditions, with no agreement ever reached or appearing reachable. It is entirely possible that they were the product of a combination of lineages from both these royal kindreds, or alternatively of another origin entirely.

Eochu Liathán ("Eochu the Grey"), son of [Dáire Cerbba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1ire_Cerbba), is the ancestor after which the Uí Liatháin is named.

The small village of [Castlelyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlelyons) (Caisleán Ó Liatháin) in East [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork) preserves the name of one of their last royal seats in the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages), as does the name of [Killaliathan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killaliathan_Church), County Limerick.

The two most powerful septs of the _Uí Liatháin_ were the **Uí Meic Caille** (including the _Uí Anmchada_) and the **Uí Thassaig** (later known as the _Uí Meic Tire_). Following the [Norman invasion of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Ireland), the _Uí Meic Caille_ gave their name to the [barony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barony_(Ireland)) of [Imokilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imokilly).

## Career in Britain

See also: [Scoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoti)

The Uí Liatháin are known from both Irish and British sources, respectively the [Sanas Cormaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanas_Cormaic) and [Historia Brittonum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historia_Brittonum), to have had colonies in [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales) and [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall). According to the _Historia Brittonum_ they were driven out of [North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales) by [Cunedda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunedda) and his sons, and possibly have settled in South Wales such as the kingdoms of [Dyfed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dyfed), [Ystrad Tywi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ystrad_Tywi), and [Brycheiniog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brycheiniog) since they weren't driven out from there.

Alongside the Uí Liatháin in this region of Britain were a significant force of the so-called [Déisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9isi), whose story is told in the famous _Expulsion of the Déisi_ already mentioned above, as well as a smaller population of the [Laigin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laigin). Neither are specifically connected to the Uí Liatháin, or connected to each other, in any of the Irish sources, but collaboration can certainly not be ruled out, especially in matters relating to trade, including the slave trade. The Déisi Muman lived adjacent to them in the neighbouring [County Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Waterford) and the Laigin could be found not much farther east in the Kingdom of [Leinster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster).

The Uí Liatháin can, however, be associated easily with their apparent relation [Crimthann mac Fidaig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimthann_mac_Fidaig), the legendary [King of Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Munster) and dominant [High King of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland) of the 4th century. They are mentioned not only in the same passage in the _Sanas Cormaic_, but are close relations in all the earliest genealogical manuscripts.

In a 1926 paper, [Eoin MacNeill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eoin_MacNeill) discusses the movements of the Uí Liatháin at considerable length, arguing their leadership in the South Irish conquests and founding of the later dynasty of [Brycheiniog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brycheiniog), figures in the Welsh genealogies matching Uí Liatháin dynasts in the Irish genealogies. He argues any possible settlement of the Déisi would have been subordinate until the ousting of the Uí Liatháin by the sons of Cunedda. The founder of Brycheiniog, [Brychan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brychan), is in all probability the early dynast Macc Brocc (for whom see below), while the name Braccan also occurs early in the pedigrees of the Uí Fidgenti and Uí Dedaid, close kindred of the Uí Liatháin. MacNeill further associates this with the sovereignty in Ireland and conquests in Britain of their first cousin, the monarch Crimthann mac Fidaig.

## Relations

[Bressal mac Ailello Thassaig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bressal_mac_Ailello_Thassaig) was an early king of Munster according to one source. His sister [Angias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angias) was the queen of [Lóegaire mac Néill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B3egaire_mac_N%C3%A9ill), [High King of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland), and mother of [Lugaid mac Lóegairi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugaid_mac_L%C3%B3egairi), who later became High King himself despite the initial wishes of [Saint Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick), thanks to Angias' beseeching the saint. She and Bressal were children of (Ailill) Tassach, son of Eochu Liathán.

[Ruithchern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruithchern?action=edit&redlink=1), daughter of the King of [Iarmuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iarmuman) (and possibly Munster), [Áed Bennán mac Crimthainn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ed_Benn%C3%A1n_mac_Crimthainn), and sister of [Mór Muman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B3r_Muman), was taken captive by the Uí Liatháin and forced to herd sheep.

At the [Battle of Carn Conaill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Carn_Conaill?action=edit&redlink=1), the Uí Liatháin are listed among the Munster allies of [Guaire Aidne mac Colmáin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaire_Aidne_mac_Colm%C3%A1in), a mention dismissed by Byrne, but discussed at length by Seán Ó Coileáin, who relates it to the cycle of Mór Muman and Ruithchern.

Both the mother, Gormgel, and celebrated wife, [Caillech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caillech?action=edit&redlink=1), of the infamous [Cathal mac Finguine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathal_mac_Finguine), [King of Munster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Munster) and [King of Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Tara), were from the Uí Liatháin.

## Later centuries

A substantial part of the recently defunct kingdom was granted to the [De Barry family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Barry_family) by [John of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_England) in 1206, although the Uí Meic Tire persisted in a southern outpost for a few decades following.

## Septs and surnames

The two most prominent septs of the Uí Liatháin were the following:

### Uí Meic Caille

The Uí Meic Caille (Uí Maic Caille), who gave their name to the [Barony of Imokilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlemartyr), took their name from Meic Caille, son of Meic Brócc, son of Eochu Liathán.

- Uí Meic Caille (O'Kiely, Kiely, Keily, Kiley, Keely, Kealy, Quelly)
- **Uí Anmchada (Lane, Lyons, Lehane)** ← _the Lyons surname belongs to this sept_
- Uí Glaissín (Glashan, Gleason, Gleeson)
- Uí Charráin (Ó Corráin, Curran)

### Uí Thassaig

These were the descendants of the aforementioned Ailill Tassach, son of Eochu Liatháin.

- Uí Meic Tire (Ó Mic Thíre, Mictyre, MacTyre, MacTire, Wolfe, Woulfe)
