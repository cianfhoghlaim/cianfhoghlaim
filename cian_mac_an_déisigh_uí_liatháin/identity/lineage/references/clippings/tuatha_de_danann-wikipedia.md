# Tuatha Dé Danann

The **Tuatha Dé Danann** ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): _[Tuatha Dé Danann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann)_, meaning "the folk of the goddess [Danu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danu_(Irish_goddess))") are the supernatural race of [pre-Christian Irish mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Christian_Irish_mythology), often interpreted as the gods of the [Gaelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaels) world. They are described in the _[Lebor Gabála Érenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebor_Gab%C3%A1la_%C3%89renn)_ ("Book of Invasions") as having inhabited Ireland before the arrival of the [Milesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milesians_(Irish)), the ancestors of the modern Irish.

## Origin and arrival

According to medieval Irish tradition, the Tuatha Dé Danann were the **fifth and last** of the mythological races to invade Ireland, after the [Cessair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessair), the [Partholón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthol%C3%B3n), the [Nemedians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemedians), and the [Fomorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomorians). They came to Ireland "in dark clouds" from four northern cities: **Falias**, **Gorias**, **Finias**, and **Murias** (sometimes understood as the four cities of the underworld or of the north of the world). From each city they brought a magical treasure:

- From **Falias** — the [Lia Fáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lia_F%C3%A1il) ("Stone of Destiny"), which would roar under the feet of the rightful High King of Ireland
- From **Gorias** — the **spear of Lugh**, which never missed its mark
- From **Finias** — the **sword of Nuada** (later wielded by Lugh as Caladbolg)
- From **Murias** — the **cauldron of the Dagda**, which could feed an entire army

## The two Battles of Mag Tuired

The Tuatha Dé Danann fought two epic battles against the [Fomorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomorians) at a site called [Mag Tuired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mag_Tuired) (now [Moytura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moytura), Co. Sligo). The **First Battle of Mag Tuired** was fought during their arrival, and the **Second Battle of Mag Tuired** was the climactic battle in which the Tuatha Dé Danann, under the leadership of the newly-arrived **[Lugh Lámhfhada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugh_L%C3%A9mhfhada)** ("Lugh of the Long Arm"), decisively defeated the Fomorians and their high king **[Balor of the Evil Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balor)** (Balor was killed by Lugh, who struck out his eye with a sling-stone).

Lugh was the son of **Cian** (the son of [Dian Cecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Cecht)) and **[Ethniu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethniu)** (the daughter of Balor). The union that produced Lugh thus united the Tuatha Dé Danann and the Fomorians in a single lineage — the cycle of violence between the two peoples would ultimately be broken only by the birth of Lugh himself.

## Defeat by the Milesians

The Tuatha Dé Danann were eventually defeated by the **Milesians** (the ancestors of the modern Irish), the sons of [Míl Espáine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%ADl_Esp%C3%A1ine). At the moment of their defeat, the Tuatha Dé Danann were divided in their counsel. Some (including the [Dagda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagda)) wished to fight the Milesians to the death; others (including [Bodb Derg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodb_Derg)) wished to withdraw into the _sídhe_ and become the hidden folk of Ireland. The latter counsel prevailed, and the Tuatha Dé Danann withdrew into the [burial mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_mound) (the _[sídhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%ADdhe)_) of Ireland, where they became the **[Aos Sí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aos_S%C3%AD)** ("folk of the mounds") of medieval and modern Irish folklore.

## The Trí Dé Dána and the principal deities

The principal deities of the Tuatha Dé Danann include:

- **The Dagda** ("the Good God") — father-figure, possessor of the cauldron of plenty
- **[Lugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugh)** (Lug, Lúcifer) — samildanach, master of all arts, the "many-skilled one"
- **[Brigid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigid)** (Brigit) — goddess of poetry, healing, and smithcraft
- **Dian Cecht** — the physician of the Tuatha Dé, father of **Cian**, **Cu**, **Cethen**, and **Miach**
- **Miach** — healer, slain by his father Dian Cecht for surpassing him in medicine
- **The Morrígan** (Morrígu, "the Phantom Queen") — goddess of war and death
- **Ogma** — god of eloquence and learning, credited with inventing the [Ogham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogham) script
- **Nuada Airgetlám** ("Nuada of the Silver Hand") — first high king, lost his hand in the First Battle of Mag Tuired and was given a silver prosthesis
- **Manannán mac Lir** — god of the sea

The **Trí Dé Dána** ("Three Gods of Craft") are **Brigid** (poetry and healing), **Dian Cecht** (medicine), and **Ogma** (eloquence and learning). Cian, as Dian Cecht's son, is a member of this craftsman's lineage by direct descent.
