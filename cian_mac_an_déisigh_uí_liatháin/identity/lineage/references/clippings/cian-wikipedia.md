# Cian

In [Irish mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_mythology), **Cian** or **Cían** ([Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) pronunciation:[ciənˠ]), nicknamed **Scal Balb**, was the son of [Dian Cecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dian_Cecht), the physician of the [Tuatha Dé Danann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuatha_D%C3%A9_Danann), and is best known as the father of [Lugh Lámhfhada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugh_L%C3%A9mhfhada) ("Lugh of the Long Arm"). Cían's brothers were **Cu**, **Cethen**, and [Miach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miach).

Cían was slain by the **Sons of Tuireann** ([Brian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_(Sons_of_Tuireann)), [Iuchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iuchar), and [Iucharba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iucharba)), for which Lugh demanded various treasures from around the world as [éraic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89raic) (compensation), according to the account in the "[Book of Invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebor_Gab%C3%A1la_%C3%89renn)" (_Lebor Gabála Érenn_, LGE) as well as the late romance version _The Fate of the Children of Tuireann_.

## Name

**Cían** means **"enduring one"**; _cían_ signifying "long, enduring, far, distant". This etymology matches the Cianfhoghlaim (cian fhoglaim) construction: _cian_ ("enduring") + _foglaim_ ("learning") = "enduring learning".

**Scal Balb** is a nickname borne by other personages and means "dumb champion", with "dumb" in the archaic sense of "unable to make speech" (i.e. mute), not in the modern sense of "stupid". The nickname reflects the romantic archetype of the mute warrior who triumphs through martial prowess rather than rhetoric.

## Parentage and family

Cían was the son of **Dian Cecht** (the physician of the Tuatha Dé Danann) and his wife **Cethniu**. His brothers were **Cu** (a warrior), **Cethen**, and **Miach** (a healer who was slain by his father Dian Cecht for surpassing him in medicine). Through Dian Cecht, Cian was a grandson of the Tuatha Dé Danann deity **Dian Cecht's father**, and a great-grandson of the Tuatha Dé Danann high king **Nuada Airgetlám**.

By most accounts, Lugh's mother was the [Fomorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomorians) princess **Ethniu** (also spelled **Ethliu** or **Edna**), daughter of the Fomorian high king **Balor of the Evil Eye**. According to the _Lebor Gabála Érenn_, Cian met Ethniu when she was abducted by the Tuatha Dé Danann and given to Cian as a wife (or consort) to seal an alliance between the two peoples. Their union produced **Lugh**, who would later lead the Tuatha Dé Danann to victory over the Fomorians at the **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**.

## Death and éraic

After the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, the Sons of Tuireann killed Cian (in some versions, through trickery; in others, in open combat). Lugh, who by then had become the high king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, demanded the **éraic** (a ritual compensation in early Irish law) for his father's death. The _Fate of the Children of Tuireann_ (_Oidheadh Chloinne Tuireann_) lists the treasures Lugh demanded, which the Sons of Tuireann were forced to seek around the world:

- The skin of the [pig of Dobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_of_Dobar) that could heal wounds
- The skin of the [heifer of Dobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heifer_of_Dobar)
- The spear of [Assal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assal) that never missed its mark
- The chariot of the king of [Sidrach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidrach)
- The [sword of Caladbolg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caladbolg) wielded by [Tethra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethra)
- The [seven pigs of Easmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easmal) that could be slaughtered and eaten daily and would regenerate
- The whelp of the king of [Ioruaidh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ioruaidh)
- The cooking spit of the woman of [Innis Cera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innis_Cera)
- The helmet and breastplate of the king of [Clochur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clochur)
- Three apples from the garden of [the Hesperides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesperides)
- The [pigskin bag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigskin_bag) that held the waters of a healing well
- The feather of the [bird of Crannog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crannog) that could turn dead warriors back to life

The Sons of Tuireann, after gathering all the treasures, returned them to Lugh and were permitted to live — but only after the three brothers themselves were fatally wounded in the quest, and Lugh reluctantly allowed them a final death by their father Tuireann rather than denying his father-in-law the right to bury his sons.

## Modern application

In the Cianfhoghlaim repository name, the **cian** element explicitly references the etymology of this Tuatha Dé Danann figure ("the enduring one"), the father of Lugh. The Cianfhoghlaim code-identifier is thus grounded in Irish mythological genealogy: Cian (Dian Cecht's son) → Lugh (samildanach, master of all arts) → the threefold gift of knowledge, skill, and prophecy that defines the Cianfhoghlaim dataset platform.
