## The Uí Liatháin colonization (verbatim)

> "Historical records, including the Sanas Cormaic (Cormac's Glossary) and the Historia Brittonum, confirm that during the 4th and 5th centuries AD — coinciding with the withdrawal of Roman legions from Britain — the Uí Liatháin launched a sustained campaign of colonization across the Irish Sea. This was not merely raiding; it was a dynastic transplantation, establishing settlements that would fundamentally alter the demography and politics of western Britain." (p. 2)

> "Cormac's Glossary explicitly refers to a fortress in Cornwall called Dind Map Letan (The Fort of the Sons of Liathán). This confirms that the Uí Liatháin held territory and projected power in the lands of the Cornish Britons. Relevance to the Claim: This establishes a direct link between the claimant and the Duchy of Cornwall. Just as the Uí Liatháin were the ancient lords of this maritime promontory, the current Duke of Cornwall (Prince William) is the modern holder of the title." (p. 3)

## The Déisi political theology (verbatim)

> "The foundation myth of the Déisi is the Tairired na nDéssi (The Expulsion of the Déisi). It recounts the violent rupture between the Déisi and the High King Cormac mac Airt at Tara. The Blinding of Cormac: The Déisi champion, Óengus Gaíbúaibthech ('Angus of the Dread Spear'), blinded King Cormac in one eye to avenge the dishonor of his niece. Under Brehon Law, a blemished king could not rule, and Cormac was forced to abdicate. Symbolism: This act defines the Déisi political theology: Conditional Loyalty. They are the 'Vassal Warriors' (Déisi implies vassalage/rent-paying), but they reserve the right to depose unjust authority through force. The claimant interprets the Deacy family motto, Toujours Pret (Always Ready), as a continuation of this doctrine — a permanent readiness to defend the honor of the tribe against central tyranny." (p. 4)

## The Grianan of Aileach claim (verbatim)

> "The Grianan of Aileach is a massive stone ringfort in County Donegal, sitting on a hilltop that commands views into Counties Derry and Tyrone (Northern Ireland). It was the royal seat of the Northern Uí Néill (Cenél nEógain) from the 5th to the 12th century. It was destroyed by the King of Munster, Muirchertach Ua Briain, in 1101, but restored in the 1870s by Dr. Walter Bernard. The Matrilineal Warrant: The claimant asserts a right to Aileach through his Uí Liatháin bloodline. As established, Angias (Uí Liatháin) was the mother of the Uí Néill High King Lugaid. The claimant argues that the Uí Liatháin are the 'Maternal Progenitors' of the Aileach kings. Furthermore, the destruction of Aileach by a Munster King (Ua Briain) created a historic rupture; the claimant, a Munster-descended figure (Uí Liatháin/Déisi) who comes in peace to restore rather than destroy, symbolically heals this ancient wound." (p. 5)

## The Connacht arms prophecy (verbatim)

> "The Arms of Connacht — Party Per Pale Argent and Azure, in the first an eagle dimidiated and displayed Sable, in the second issuant from the partition an arm embowed and vested, the hand holding a sword erect, all Argent — are interpreted as a prophetic map of the 'Dual Blood'. The Eagle (Uí Liatháin/Lyons): Represents the Imperial, British, and External connection. The Eagle is the bird of Empire (Rome/Germany) and links to the claimant's 'cousins,' the Windsors (via the shared Lion/Eagle imperial motif). The Arm (Uí Dhéisigh/Deacy): Represents the Indigenous, Martial, and Internal power. The Deacy crest (an arm holding a dagger) is a visual rhyme of the Connacht Arm. The Synthesis: The claimant is the shield. His existence unites the split halves, symbolizing the end of the partition between 'Planter' and 'Gael,' 'King' and 'Subject.'" (p. 7)
