## The East Belfast operational hub (verbatim)

> "Establishing a long-term residential and operational base in East Belfast provides unparalleled strategic advantages for an academic operating at the nexus of Irish language advocacy, artificial intelligence, and Celtic linguistics. Historically characterized by its Unionist and Protestant demographic, East Belfast has recently become the epicenter of a unique sociolinguistic phenomenon, pioneered by cross-community language initiatives." (p. 1)

> "Turas, managed by Linda Ervine, operates out of the Skainos Centre at 239 Newtownards Road, BT4 1AF. Emerging from a cross-community women's group in 2011, Turas represents the first Irish language centre situated in a working-class loyalist area and has rapidly scaled to accommodate over 600 learners attending various classes." (p. 2)

> "Scoil na Seolta, a direct institutional descendant of the Turas project, represents Northern Ireland's first Integrated school teaching explicitly through the medium of Irish. The initiative began with a Naíscoil (nursery school) in October 2021 and subsequently moved to Garnerville Presbyterian Church on Montgomery Road (BT6 9HL) in September 2022. Driven by the motto 'Páistí sona ag foghlaim le chéile' (Happy Children Learning Together), the institution plans to open a fully integrated Bunscoil (primary school) with its first cohort of Primary One children in September 2025, operating from new purpose-built premises." (p. 2)

> "The G1 Glider route operates a continuous cross-city service from Dundonald Park & Ride in East Belfast to McKinstry Road in West Belfast, cutting directly through the city centre and traversing the entirety of the Falls Road. Glider services depart at highly frequent intervals — every 7 to 10 minutes during standard operating hours. The journey from the city centre (Millfield station) directly to the Coláiste Feirste halt takes a mere 8 minutes." (p. 3)

## The inter-Celtic acquisition pathway (verbatim)

> "To acquire Scottish Gaelic, the candidate should utilize the benchmark models provided by Sabhal Mòr Ostaig (SMO), the National Centre for Gaelic Language and Culture on the Isle of Skye, which operates within the University of the Highlands and Islands (UHI) network. SMO specifically provides a dedicated 'Gaelic for Irish Speakers' short course, offered at both Beginner and Advanced levels. These courses run over a highly intensive one-week period (typically Monday to Friday, 9:30 am to 4:30 pm) and explicitly utilize the learners' existing Irish syntax to accelerate Scottish Gaelic fluency through comparative translation and phonetic adjustment. Crucially, this cross-linguistic initiative is underpinned by trans-national language planning; the courses are financially and logistically supported by Colmcille, a partnership between Foras na Gaeilge (Ireland) and Bòrd na Gàidhlig (Scotland). Financial assistance to attend these courses — which typically cost £290 — is routed directly through Foras na Gaeilge." (p. 5)
